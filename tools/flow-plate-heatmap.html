Plate Heatmap Generator

Turn a table of statistics into a plate map. Load a CSV from flow cytometry software or a plate reader, choose a variable, and every well is colored by its value on a 96- or 384-well plate, with the sample name on the well. From there you can normalize to reference wells, rank the samples in a bar chart, group replicates with standard deviation error bars, and save the figures. Everything runs in your browser. Your file is never uploaded.

  1. Load your data. Choose a CSV, or try an example. Each row is a sample and each column a statistic.
  2. Check the plate. Pick 96 or 384 wells and how samples are placed. Fix any that landed in the wrong well by dragging.
  3. Pick a variable. The dropdown lists every numeric column. Colors, normalization and the chart all follow it.
  4. Refine and save. Set colors and labels under the plate, group replicates under the chart, then download.
Everything this tool can do
Files it reads
Flow cytometry tables with one row per file, including FlowJo exports that have two header rows. Plate reader files in plate-grid layout (a row of 1–12 with rows A–H below, one or more blocks, each block becomes a variable) or list layout with a Well column. Comma, semicolon and tab separated files work, as do comma decimals. Rows named Mean, SD or CV are skipped. If the header is misread, change “Header row”.
Placing samples
“In file order” fills wells from a start well, across rows or down columns. “From a well column” and “From a well ID inside the sample name” read positions such as A1 or B07. The tool switches to 384 wells on its own when the data needs it. Changing these settings rebuilds the layout and clears manual moves.
Moving samples
Drag a well onto another to move its sample, or swap if that well is taken. Samples that do not fit on the plate, or that you take off it, wait in the tray under the plate, so drag them back when you want them. You can also select a well and choose its sample from the list.
Selecting several wells
Ctrl/Cmd-click to add or remove wells, Shift-click for a block, or click a row letter or column number for a whole line. With the keyboard, arrow keys move, Space toggles, Shift with arrows extends, Ctrl/Cmd+A selects all. Use the bar above the plate to move the selection off the plate (Delete works too) and undo it, or to group the wells as replicates.
Normalizing
Press “Pick reference wells” and click your controls (or a row letter or column number). Choose fold change, percent of reference, difference, or log2 fold change. Several reference wells are averaged for each variable. The “ref” mark follows the sample if you move it. Normalized values drive the colors, labels, chart and exports.
Colors
Pick a standard scale, or a fluorophore (FITC, PE, PE-Texas Red, APC, BV421, DAPI) so its color is the maximum. Change either end color to build your own, set a minimum and maximum, use a log scale (important for MFI), or reverse the colors.
Well labels
Show the sample name and value, either one, or nothing. You can hide “.fcs” and change the well size.
Ranked bar chart
Every sample on the plate is ranked from highest to lowest with labeled axes. Bars use the heatmap colors, reference wells are outlined, and hovering shows the details. It follows the variable, normalization and log scale, and moving a sample off the plate removes it from the chart.
Replicate groups
Under the chart, group replicates by sample name (for example C1_d100 and C1_d100(1)), by neighboring wells (such as triplicates A1–A3), or by hand with “Group as replicates”. Bars then show the group mean with error bars for the standard deviation (n − 1) or standard error, and you can add a dot for each replicate. Loading a new file clears the groups.
Saving
Download the plate as a PNG with its color legend, a layout CSV (well, sample, value, and normalization details), the chart as PNG or SVG, and the group statistics as a CSV.
Privacy
Your data stays in this browser tab. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is kept between visits, so reload and your file is gone.
