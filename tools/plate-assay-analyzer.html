Plate Assay Analyzer

Turn a table of statistics into a plate map. Load a CSV from flow cytometry software or a plate reader, choose a variable, and every well is colored by its value on a 96- or 384-well plate, with the sample name on the well. From there you can normalize to reference wells, rank the samples in a bar chart, group replicates with standard deviation error bars, and save the figures. Everything runs in your browser. Your file is never uploaded.

  1. Load your data. Choose a CSV, or try an example. Each row is a sample and each column a statistic.
  2. Check the plate. Pick 96 or 384 wells and how samples are placed. Fix any that landed in the wrong well by dragging.
  3. Pick a variable. The dropdown lists every numeric column. Colors, normalization and the chart all follow it.
  4. Refine and save. Set colors and labels under the plate, group replicates under the chart, then download.
Everything this tool can do
Files it reads
Flow cytometry tables with one row per file, including FlowJo exports that have two header rows. Plate reader files in plate-grid layout (a row of 1–12 with rows A–H below, one or more blocks, each block becomes a variable) or list layout with a Well column. Comma, semicolon and tab separated files work, as do comma decimals. Rows named Mean, SD or CV are skipped. If the header is misread, change “Header row”.
Placing samples
“In file order” fills wells from a start well, across rows or down columns. “From a well column” and “From a well ID inside the sample name” read positions such as A1 or B07. The tool switches to 384 wells on its own when the data needs it. Changing these settings rebuilds the layout and clears manual moves.
Moving samples
Drag a well onto another to move its sample, or swap if that well is taken. Samples that do not fit on the plate, or that you take off it, wait in the tray under the plate, so drag them back when you want them. You can also select a well and choose its sample from the list.
Selecting several wells
Ctrl/Cmd-click picks wells one at a time. Shift-click selects every well from your last click to the one you click, carrying on to the next row, so A12 then B1 selects just those two. A row letter or column number selects that whole line. A bar then appears above the plate to move the selection off the plate (or press Delete), group it as replicates, or mark it as controls. Extras: Shift+Alt-click selects a rectangle, and with the keyboard the arrow keys move, Space toggles a well, Shift+arrows extend, and Ctrl/Cmd+A selects all.
Normalizing
Press “Pick reference wells” and click your controls (or a row letter or column number). Choose fold change, percent of reference, difference, or log2 fold change. Several reference wells are averaged for each variable. The “ref” mark follows the sample if you move it. “Percent of positive control” instead scales each well between your negative (0%) and positive (100%) controls. Normalized values drive the colors, labels, chart and exports. Normalizing rewrites the numbers, so every well becomes relative to your reference wells. Controls do not change the numbers; they judge them, by measuring assay quality and setting the bins and cutoffs. “Percent of positive control” is the one place where controls rescale the data.
Controls and analyses
Mark negative and positive control wells (the Controls panel, or select wells and use the bar above the plate). Under “Statistical analyses” below the chart you then get control quality (Z′-factor, stain index, signal-to-background, CV), bins that sort every well by how many SDs it sits from each control (and can color the plate), a robust z-score that needs no controls, and a two-variable scatter with control cutoffs. Each section collapses, and each explains what it measures. Control wells are shaded on the plate: blue hatching for negative controls, red crosshatching for positive controls, and gray lines for normalization reference wells.
Colors
Pick a standard scale, or a fluorophore (FITC, PE, PE-Texas Red, APC, BV421, DAPI) so its color is the maximum. Change either end color to build your own, set a minimum and maximum, use a log scale (important for MFI), or reverse the colors.
Well labels
Show the sample name and value, either one, or nothing. You can hide “.fcs” and change the well size.
Ranked chart
Every sample on the plate is ranked from highest to lowest with labeled axes. Choose a bar chart or a box-and-whisker plot (box = first to third quartile, line = median, whiskers = minimum to maximum or 1.5 × IQR with outliers as dots). Colors follow the heatmap, reference wells are outlined, and hovering shows the details. It follows the variable, normalization and log scale, and moving a sample off the plate removes it from the chart.
Replicate groups
Under the chart, group replicates by sample name (for example C1_d100 and C1_d100(1)), by neighboring wells (such as triplicates A1–A3), or by hand with “Group as replicates”. Bars then show the group mean with error bars for the standard deviation (n − 1) or standard error, and you can add a dot for each replicate. Every group is listed above the chart: tick or untick it to add it to or remove it from the chart, and edit its name to rename it (clear the name to restore the original). Loading a new file clears the groups.
Saving
Download the plate as a PNG with its color legend, a layout CSV (well, sample, value, and normalization details), the chart as PNG or SVG, and the group statistics as a CSV.
Privacy
Your data stays in this browser tab. Nothing is uploaded and nothing is kept between visits, so reload and your file is gone.

    Statistical analyses

    Optional. Collapse any section with the arrow in its corner. They use the variable chosen above.

    Control qualityZ′-factor, stain index, signal-to-background and CV: do your controls separate cleanly?
    Control binsSort wells by how many SDs they sit from your negative and positive controls.

    A bin is a category a well falls into. The tool measures the spread of your control wells, then sorts every well by where it lands: inside the negative band (the negative mean ± k SD), inside the positive band, between the two (intermediate), or beyond. With only negative controls, wells past the negative band are called hits. Three SDs is the usual hit cutoff. With only a few control wells the SD is uncertain, so aim for eight or more, or choose the robust spread above.

    Robust z-scoreHow unusual each well is compared with the rest of the plate, with no controls needed.

    The robust z-score is (value − plate median) ÷ (1.4826 × MAD), where MAD is the median absolute deviation. Because it uses medians, a few strong hits do not distort it the way they would a mean and SD. Use it when most wells are expected to be inactive. A score beyond ±3 is a common hit or outlier cutoff. It is not meaningful on plates where most wells are treated, such as a full dose series.

    Two-variable scatterPlot two statistics against each other, such as % positive against MFI, with control cutoffs.

    Each well is a point. Dashed lines mark the negative-control cutoff (mean ± k SD) on each axis, which splits the points into four quadrants: beyond both cutoffs, beyond one, or neither. Control wells are ringed. The scatter uses each variable’s raw values, not the normalization.
